2. Observation and motion consume shift time

A Phase 2 step calculates tray movement and picking time, then checks the drop draw. The camera version feeds pockets, scans with the wrist camera, moves and settles, picks, checks output, and removes the object. Waiting jobs accumulate lateness during these operations.

The fast camera model matches measured rows by mass, handling risk, pocket, handling mode, and tray movement. Physics evaluation simulates contact and friction, and records camera detection and picking failures.

Camera failures, retries, and recovery consume time and affect completion counts. Read those diagnostics alongside improvement over each environment's baselines.

The 480-second cutoff also differs. Phase 2's step calculates a started job's finish time, which can exceed the shift end. The camera version passes the remaining time budget to the executor, interrupts work that exceeds it, and counts that job as unfinished.

The camera score uses simulated physics time. Rendering and process communication time are excluded.

5. Solver objectives and references

Phase 2

  • Use the realized processing-time noise.
  • Remove drops and careful handling.
  • Keep tray movement, lateness, and unfinished-job costs.
  • Maximize score.

Record a feasible schedule's value clairvoyant and the proven upper bound bound separately.

Camera workcell

  • Use the minimum durations available in measured samples.
  • Remove scan, travel, retry, and failure constraints.
  • Remove lateness, travel, drop, and unfinished-job costs from the objective.
  • Maximize on-time completions.

Multiply the upper bound on on-time completions by the reward to obtain a fast-model score ceiling.

The camera solver's short durations come from the fast model's sample set. Physical execution can be faster or slower than those samples. This bound does not establish an optimality gap for camera physics results.

The code's physics_score_upper_bound multiplies total job count by the on-time reward. It is a separate, simple ceiling.

Cumulative Holm adjustment

for rank, index in enumerate(order):
    maximum = max(maximum, (len(values)-rank)*values[index])
    adjusted[index] = min(1., maximum)

For example probability values 0.010, 0.040, and 0.041, rank multipliers alone produce 0.030, 0.080, and 0.041. A cumulative maximum produces 0.030, 0.080, and 0.080. This can change the decision for the last comparison.

These values are illustrative, not test results. Phase 2 statistics code is unchanged.

7. Runtime, resume, and outputs

  • Runtime: Phase 2 uses a standard training environment. The camera version uses Python 3.12 and Isaac Sim 6.0.0.1 worker processes on the same Linux or Windows computer, with a supported NVIDIA graphics card.
  • Small runs: Phase 2's smoke uses one seed, 3,000 steps, three validation shifts, and five test shifts. The camera version's quick uses one seed, 2,048 steps, two validation shifts, and two test shifts, with 30-second shifts and two main conditions.
  • Settings cells: Phase 2 selects settings from the PROFILES dictionary. The camera version runs a full-settings cell, then a separate small-run override cell.
  • Resume: Full runs save resume state every 10,000 steps in Phase 2 and every 25,000 steps in the camera version. The camera version also restores the fast environment and execution model state, and checks configuration and measurement hashes. This does not resume a running physics simulation.
  • Extra outputs: Measurement tables, shared orders, physics validation scores, camera/reference-position gaps, and videos from both cameras.
